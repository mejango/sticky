# Risks

Sticky permanently binds projects to a specific core release and underlying token. A correct accounting implementation does not establish the safety of those dependencies, the suitability of a reward program, or a live deployment. Read this with [INVARIANTS.md](./INVARIANTS.md) and [ADMINISTRATION.md](./ADMINISTRATION.md).

## Priority risks

| Risk | Consequence | Integration requirement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Maximum cash out tax | Every redemption returns zero, including a whole-supply exit | Show this permanent outcome before launch and before a holder stakes |
| Snapshot reward capture | Temporary ownership can retain group-0 rewards after exiting | Treat group-0 rewards as balance-at-a-block allocations; fund a tenure group when age should matter |
| Tenure claims need a live position | Exiting before claiming forfeits a tenure allocation to the pot | Claim tenure rewards before unstaking; tell holders the claim window |
| Unsupported underlying token | Transfer failures, backing mismatch, or unexpected value loss | Review transfer, rebase, freeze, mint, and upgrade behavior before accepting an asset |
| Incorrect core or deployment identity | Immutable bindings can point to unsuitable or privileged dependencies | Verify code, bindings, chain, and executed receipts for the reviewed release |
| Incorrect bridge destination | Rewards can become inaccessible or fund unintended holders | Predict the receiver on the destination chain and verify the reward-token route |
| Incorrect collector binding or split configuration | Incorrect dependencies or a nonexistent destination can strand rewards; a rejected hook callback can burn ERC-20 reserves under core's catch-and-burn behavior | Verify the shared hook and destination, then use the same reviewed hook/beneficiary/group configuration on every source |
| Credits sent to an ERC-20-only reward destination | The distributor and reward receiver cannot claim project credits | Use the collector to queue source credits; ensure the home-chain reward ERC-20 exists before a remote claim |
| Unattributed Arbitrum transport refund | A raw refund can be contributed to protocol fee project 1 instead of returned to its payer; a positive mapped-ERC-20 gateway refund can remain at the fee child's alias | Bound transport budgets, verify the matching raw-address contribution, and keep aliased gateway backing unqualified |
| Arbitrum WETH gateway cancellation or expiry | Bridged call value can credit `alias(source sucker)`, which Sticky cannot spend | Keep positive WETH backing unqualified pending an explicit reviewed destination or alias recovery |

## Redemption and deposit availability

The cash out tax is a curve parameter, not a flat percentage deducted from every exit. Below the maximum, redeeming the whole supply returns all share-owned backing before terminal fees. At the maximum, gross reclaim is zero. A voluntary burn never returns backing. Another holder's dust does not prevent a full holder exit.

The terminal's `previewCashOutFrom` reports gross reclaim. A non-feeless beneficiary owes the terminal fee against all reclaim at positive tax, or against the reclaim covered by `feeFreeSurplusOf` at zero tax. A failed fee route credits the fee back to project backing; it does not refund the beneficiary. Client minimums must protect the net amount actually expected.

Share issuance rounds down and rejects excessive rounding loss. Initial issuance has a minimum, but later burns can leave very small supplies. Large backing per share atom, including after a donation, can make small deposits preview zero and revert. This preserves the payer's funds and exit independence; it does not guarantee that every desired deposit remains possible. The share-rounding guard does not bound underlying-token redemption rounding or protocol fees.

Donations to an empty project and backing left after the last voluntary burn are unowned. Subsequent shares cannot reclaim them. There is no rescue or administrator withdrawal path for orphaned backing.

Value added to a Sticky project's balance through `addToBalanceOf` or a `preferAddToBalance` payout split accrues immediately to current holders, so a holder who enters just before such an inflow and exits right after captures their share of it (`test_acceptedRisk_payoutSplitInflowCapturedByTransientHolder`); projects should not route payout splits into Sticky projects.

## Reward allocation and timing

A pool's home chain is part of its identity. Same-name or same-address pools on other chains have independent supplies, backing and reward snapshots. Combining them as one pool would misstate both ownership and redemption. Reward-source availability is separate: a destination-bound collector does not prove a usable bridge lane. The pinned native JBP6/REV routes form an Ethereum hub, so L2-to-L2 delivery needs a separately qualified direct route. There is no automatic relay or fallback to Ethereum; plain transfers to an intermediate collector do not create attributed custody.

Group 0 uses ownership snapshots without a minimum stake age. First positive funding or settlement pins the current round if needed. Anyone can call `poke()` to pin both the current and next round if unset, using the previous block. That timing is shared by every project on the distributor.

A holder can acquire shares, wait one block, pin snapshots, and exit while retaining the pinned group-0 allocations. A positive cash out tax raises stake/redeem cost but cannot prevent a profitable capture when rewards exceed that cost. Transferable shares can be temporarily acquired and returned without redeeming at all. Funders who want age to matter must fund a tenure group; group 0 remains age-blind by design.

Tenure groups weigh tranche age in whole weeks, measured from the week the round started in. A stake made earlier in that same week, or after the round started, is not eligible for that round regardless of the clock; the earliest it can qualify is a round starting `minWeeks` weeks later. A funder cannot shift a round's window by funding it later in the round. Rounds start on the distributor's own schedule, not on week boundaries, so a round can span two weeks; its window is still the one pinned by its start.

A tenure denominator is read from the hook's buckets at the round's first funding. A claim reads the holder's live tranches, so a holder must still hold the qualifying tranches when they claim: exiting or transferring first forfeits that allocation, which stays in the pot and recycles into the current round after the claim window. Partial exits consume the newest tranches first, so a tenure allocation survives a partial exit that leaves the aged tranches intact, while a recency window loses eligibility from its newest tranches first. Bounded windows pay deposits, not people: a continuous staker's cohort share is only the slice deposited in that window.

The denominator read relies on the hook's buckets summing to the token supply. During a payment the terminal mints before the hook records the tranche, so the buckets lag the supply until the after-pay callback runs. The registered token reports every mint to the hook, which flags the project's payment as in progress (`isPayingFor`) in transient storage until that callback records the tranche. A tenure funding or receiver settlement that reaches the distributor in that gap reverts with `StickyDistributor_PaymentInProgress` instead of recording a denominator, and a reverted payment leaves no flag behind. Group 0 weighs a past block and is unaffected. Reaching the gap requires an underlying token with transfer callbacks, which Sticky does not support.

The round current at receiver settlement determines its recipients and, for tenure groups, its window. Anyone can settle immediately or across a round boundary. A funder cannot reserve an arrival for a chosen future round by leaving it in a receiver. Funding a round with zero eligible stake, including a tenure window nothing has aged into, leaves that round without a claimant until its unclaimed inventory becomes recyclable.

Splits carry the group in `split.projectId`. Tools that render a split destination without checking its hook first will show that number as a project. For direct distributor splits, an invalid group or an unregistered beneficiary silently funds group 0; a funder who intends a tenure group must confirm the split's `projectId` decodes as one. The shared collector instead rejects invalid groups and zero beneficiaries during acceptance. Its prediction of a nonzero token's receiver does not establish that the home-chain pool exists or can settle the chosen group.

A reserved-token split directly to the distributor only works once the source project has deployed its ERC-20. Before that, the controller moves project credits to the hook before calling it, then catches the hook's revert: those credits stay in the distributor, which has no path to claim or transfer them. The shared source collector instead accepts attributed credits and can materialize them after the source ERC-20 exists. This does not change the destination receiver: a remote claim before the home-chain reward ERC-20 exists can leave credits in a receiver that cannot recover them. Payout splits are unaffected.

Every share holder is self-delegated, contracts included, so reward weight follows balances by design. An AMM pool that holds transferable shares receives an allocation that anyone can collect to the pool and then skim. The deployer rejects wrapping a Sticky token registered with its own hook. A different deployer/hook can still accept those shares as an underlying token, leaving the terminal with reward weight and no general reward-collection path for that custody. Cross-factory nested Sticky pools are unsupported. A Sticky token funded as a reward leaves the distributor itself holding aged shares with weight in that token's rounds. The distributor cannot pay itself: collecting its allocation to the distributor, the only beneficiary a helper may use, recycles the unlocked amount into the current round of the same pot (`collectVestedRewards(..., beneficiary: distributor)`, or `releaseForfeitedRewards` for group 0), and a holder collecting their own rewards to the distributor is rejected. The distributor keeps its weight, so each recycled round hands it a share again; the remainder shrinks geometrically across rounds and anyone can keep recycling it.

Vesting starts through a transaction for eligible past allocations; elapsed time since funding alone does not mean all rewards are collectable. Auto-stick is permissionless to execute but requires someone to send transactions. Anyone can collect to the holder before a keeper, which preserves the reward in the wallet but prevents that later compound from using it. Auto-stick handles only the project's underlying reward token.

## Trust boundaries

- **Core authority.** `JBController.OMNICHAIN_RULESET_OPERATOR` can bypass owner authorization to queue rulesets. The canonical omnichain deployer checks owner/operator permissions before calling core. Verify its binding and code on each chain. Sticky's zero-duration ruleset has no approval hook to delay an incorrect privileged configuration.
- **Core forwarder and permissions.** Core transaction sender resolution and delegated authority remain part of the security model. The factory's lack of an owner-call surface is not a substitute for correct dependencies. The deployer, hook, distributor and AutoStick trust core's ERC-2771 forwarder, so a sponsor can relay launches, trust updates, auto-stick settings, claims and funding for the signer; whoever controls that forwarder can act as any holder in those calls. Terminal, token, controller and deployer callbacks keep authenticating the direct caller and never accept the forwarder.
- **Protocol controls.** Core creation fees, feeless configuration, and default price feeds retain their respective authority models. Sticky's exact post-pay check prevents a mismatched fallback price from silently changing issued shares; a failed feed can still make payments revert.
- **Underlying custody.** The terminal's internal ledger is not a guarantee against token rebases, freezes, upgrades, or malicious callbacks. Fee-on-transfer and rebasing underlying tokens are unsupported; some paths may appear to work without making the whole integration safe.
- **Project identity.** Launching is permissionless. A familiar token name, symbol, or project URI does not authenticate its underlying asset or launcher. Use chain and contract addresses from a confirmed launch event.
- **Share transfers.** In transferable mode, an unsolicited transfer can start a position without the payment trust gate. Transferred tokens join the recipient's current-week tranche or start a new one, and preserve an existing recipient's holder streak. Granters are permanent and cannot be individually revoked by holders.
- **Reward receivers.** Cross-chain address parity depends on the verified factory address and receiver implementation, the destination Sticky-token address, and the group. A Sticky token's address is CREATE2-bound to its launcher (the signer, for a relayed launch), launch configuration and project ID (`predictStickyTokenOf`), so no other launcher can occupy a predicted address; the same launcher must receive the same project ID on every chain for token addresses to match, and every launch on a chain advances that ID. Rewards routed to a receiver for a token that never deploys stay in that receiver. These contracts do not validate bridge messages or recover mistaken native-token or ERC-20 transfers to unsupported destinations. A tenure-group receiver for a token the hook does not track cannot settle; its balance stays in the receiver.
- **Source custody and routes.** The collector binds a registry, token registry, receiver factory and nonzero destination chain at construction and derives the directory from that registry. Deployment must authenticate those identities and the selected home-chain family independently. Each send checks registered route membership before trusting its getters, then verifies the source project, canonical bindings, peer on the immutable home chain, live sending state and backing mapping. Registry-approved builders, source-project controller and peer configuration, project-token behavior, terminal hooks, fee accounting and native transport remain dependencies. Source-side prediction does not establish that the intended home-chain pool or reward ERC-20 exists. Core, source project and bridge changes can affect availability and economics after deployment.

## Operational limits

`StickySourceCollector` has no owner, destination reassignment or general rescue. Its one fixed fee-child contribution adds the child's full raw native balance to project 1 on the family's home chain; no caller can choose the project, token, terminal or beneficiary. Each accepted allocation belongs to one source project, home-chain Sticky token and reward group. Acceptance is separate from delivery on the home chain as well as remote chains: unavailable routes, missing source ERC-20s, unpaid bridge fees and failed settlement do not reject an otherwise valid allocation. Invalid split configuration can still reject acceptance; core catches hook failures, burns unconsumed ERC-20 reserves, and leaves already-transferred credits at the hook without an accepted allocation. A plain transfer outside an acceptance callback creates no pending entitlement. Follow [the deployment recipe](DEPLOYMENT.md#source-collectors) before assigning funds.

Valid nested reserved callbacks remain accepted while a token transfer or fee payment is in progress. The collector measures combined ERC-20 and credit custody and subtracts nested amounts already booked. Outbound delivery is blocked during every active acceptance measurement, and outbound reentrancy is separately blocked. These checks do not make malicious, rebasing, upgradeable or freezing project tokens safe; canonical controller and token accounting remain trusted.

A delivery caller chooses a positive partial amount from one bucket and, off the home chain, a registered usable route and its mapped backing asset. It cannot change that bucket's receiver or spend another bucket. Preparation, submission, credit materialization and refund failures revert the entire delivery attempt. A retired route can be replaced by another registered route satisfying the same canonical bindings and immutable home-chain destination; if none is available, rewards stay queued. Unsupported ERC-20 donations have no rescue path. Native currency outside the fee child also has no rescue path. Preexisting or forced native currency at a fee child can be contributed only on that family's configured destination chain; on a source or other chain it cannot subsidize `send` and remains stranded. A successful native submission cannot be recalled.

The caller supplies at least the current registry fee in native wei plus any native transport budget, and pays transaction gas separately. A fee increase or insufficient transport budget can cause a retryable failure. A positive registry fee requires the fee-project ERC-20 to exist; a zero fee does not. If the fee-project ERC-20 exists, the child returns its same-call token balance increase. Independently, it claims fresh registry-fee and transport-refund credits created by this source submission directly to the original delivery caller and requires both ledgers empty before and after. A caller that rejects any receipt or refund aborts only its own attempt. The measured token increase includes deliberately timed donations but excludes preexisting token donations; it is not proof of receipt provenance. Only the parent is the reserved split hook, and the fee child must never receive source allocations.

Asynchronous destination-chain refunds are outside those source-chain ledgers. Every Ethereum-to-Arbitrum submission creates the unsafe root retryable, whose excess refund targets raw `feeChild`. If the same Arbitrum-home collector family is deployed and runtime-verified on both chains, anyone can call the Arbitrum collector to contribute the destination child's complete raw native balance to project 1 through its current primary native terminal. The original caller is not reimbursed; unattributed refunds and forced transfers on that destination intentionally share the fixed outcome. A failed terminal call preserves the balance for retry.

A positive mapped-ERC-20 transfer additionally creates a gateway retryable through the safe Inbox. That leg credits `alias(feeChild)` rather than the deployed raw child and is not recovered by this operation; a zero-backing ERC-20 send creates no gateway ticket. The canonical WETH gateway turns the bridged amount into L2 call value and uses the L1 `_from` gateway caller—the source sucker—as `callValueRefundAddress`; cancellation or expiry therefore credits `alias(source sucker)`. Current Sticky and Sucker entrypoints control neither aliased account. Keep positive ERC-20-backed lanes unqualified until Suckers supports explicit reviewed destination recipients or an independently reviewed alias recovery. A native lane still requires same-family deployment, address/runtime parity and an end-to-end refund contribution receipt before qualification. Ethereum-home JBX delivery from Arbitrum does not use either direction. Registered CCIP alternatives also exist, including direct L2 pairs; native backing does not imply native transport. Their excess native transport budget is refunded synchronously on the source, which is compatible with the child's retained-credit handling, but router/lane identity and the complete route still require separate qualification.

Route state is checked at delivery, not collector construction. `DEPRECATION_PENDING` permits sending until `deprecatedAfter - _maxMessagingDelay()`, earlier than full deprecation; `SENDING_DISABLED` and `DEPRECATED` do not. Monitor the current route and possible replacements. Changing future unlocked splits cannot redirect an already accepted bucket.

The execution-time minimum is the chosen backing terminal's gross preview less the maximum standard protocol fee, in backing-token atoms. Zero is allowed because destination project-token reminting follows the source leaf's project-token count. The minimum is not a funder's market-price limit. Caller-sensitive custom cashout hooks may cause preview/execution mismatches that fail closed. Partial sends allow upstream count limits to be respected; outbox submission can also carry prior queued leaves. Permissionless send and settlement timing can change which reward round receives an arrival. Permissionless availability supplies no automatic keeper or service guarantee: source submission, canonical finalization, destination claim, raw-fee contribution when applicable, settlement and holder collection each require execution and monitoring.

An exit debits one epoch bucket per distinct week it consumes. Same-week deposits merge, so dust cannot multiply that work, but a position built up weekly for ten years (520 tranches) costs about 3.3M gas to exit fully from cold storage, roughly 6,300 gas per week. Tenure denominators walk at most `MAX_CRITERIA_WEEKS` buckets plus the weeks elapsed since the round started.

Whole-array tranche reads are unbounded; use pagination at a pinned block. RPC failures and incomplete log ranges must be distinguished from a zero balance or absent reward. A submitted transaction is not a confirmed action: recover the canonical receipt before retrying a launch, approval, stake, or bridge operation.

Simulation manifests do not establish deployed code. Runtime changes alter CREATE2 predictions, and existing immutable projects retain their original contracts. Publish only executed, verified contract records and exercise the corresponding target-chain wallet flows before production cutover. The client’s empty collector registry does not establish a live destination family. See [DEPLOYMENT.md](./DEPLOYMENT.md).
