## Destination and custody decisions

Constructor arguments are `(registry, tokens, receiverFactory, destinationChainId)`. The last value is nonzero and immutable as `DESTINATION_CHAIN_ID`; the directory still comes from the registry. Same factory, initcode, arguments and salt produce equal collector addresses across the source chains of one family. A different home chain changes the constructor word, address and custody. The nonce-1 child remains callable only by its collector.

Keep `hook = verified collector for the pool's home chain`, `beneficiary = home-chain Sticky share token`, and `split.projectId = reward group`. The controller context owns source-project identity and the reserved-token group. The collector address namespaces home-chain custody, so `pendingOf(sourceProjectId, stickyToken, groupId)` and `totalPendingOf(sourceProjectId)` need no new key. Current-controller authentication, cached credit contexts, combined-custody measurement minus nested pending growth, outbound guards and exact positive partial debits remain required.

`settle(...)` executes only on the immutable destination and uses the existing receiver's whole-balance settlement. `send(...)` executes only elsewhere and requires a registered sending-enabled direct route whose peer chain equals that destination, with canonical registry/directory/tokens and a usable mapped backing asset. A caller may choose a qualified replacement route and amount, never another destination. Exact allowances, fresh backing quotes including zero, leaf inclusion and atomic fee/refund behavior remain unchanged.

Only qualified direct lanes are supported. There is no automatic full mesh, intermediate Ethereum fallback or implicit relay. Plain token arrivals at an intermediate collector are unattributed and cannot stand in for a relay. Source credits may wait for a source ERC-20; destination ERC-20 and pool readiness must be established before remote claims.
