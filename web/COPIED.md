### Stick and Unstick on useStepRun

Evaluated and not adopted: no wiring of JBM's `useStepRun` keeps their behavior, so both keep their own step logic (the accepted marker, the landing effect, and Unstick's record of what went through), and nothing in Sticky calls the copied hook yet. Wired into StickFlow as faithfully as the hook allows (`start` on the first press, `resume` after a stop, each send simulated at the block the run hands it), 11 of the 78 Stick tests fail, none edited:

- The run sends the next step as soon as one confirms. Sticky asks for a press per step, named on its button (Confirm & approve, Confirm & stick; Turn off auto-stick, Remove auto-stick permission, Remove auto-stick allowance, Confirm & unstick), so each wallet prompt follows the holder's own press. - The run resets the engine before it sends the next step, so a confirmed step's "View transaction ↗" leaves the dialog before the holder moves on.
- A press handled a render late, after the last step was counted, starts the same steps again: `resume` refuses a finished run and `start` takes it.

What `useStepRun` would need to carry them:

1. A step per press: stop after each confirmed step, keeping the engine's success and its link, until the flow asks for the next one.
2. A callback for each step that lands, with the step and its receipt, where it has `onFinish` for the last one only. Unstick records each step that went through for the account that sent it, says "Went through: … Not sent yet: …", and reads again after each step what that step changed (auto-stick after a teardown step, everything after the unstick).
3. A run that can continue a plan made again: Unstick's Retry plans again from the chain, without what went through and with a fresh quote, so the steps it goes on with are a new array, and `resume` answers only for the steps the run started with. With 2, `clear()` and `start(newSteps)` would do, since the flow would keep the record itself.
4. A finished run that refuses to start again.

JBM's flows: its MoveFlow (approve, prepare, send to the remote chain) is a step per press with its own advance effect (`advancedFor`), so with 1 and 2 it could run on the same hook instead of its own effect. Its account guard went to the engine in #103 (Task W1-S2).
